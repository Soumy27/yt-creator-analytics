Findings ledger

accepted & rejected

Every title effect is re-tested inside six age brackets. Older videos have had longer to accumulate views, and title habits are not spread evenly across time — so an effect that only appears when ages are pooled is an artifact of age, not a property of the title. A finding is kept only if its direction holds in every bracket. Bars rise from the 1.00× rule where the element helps and fall where it hurts; entries that reverse are struck from the record.

Early velocity

Does a video's first day predict its ceiling? The API returns only today's view count and keeps no history, so this question can only be answered for videos published while the collector runs. It is accumulating now, every six hours.

collector timeline

An earlier build of this analysis reported a perfect correlation — r² = 1.00, “early views explain 100% of final views.” It was comparing a number to itself: with one day of data, the “24-hour” and “latest” readings were the same snapshot. The giveaway was a video showing more than 100% of its final views arriving on day one. The test now refuses to run until the readings are genuinely far apart.

Read this before quoting any number

  • Performance means views ÷ subscribers. The public API exposes no click-through rate, watch time or audience retention — those require owning the channel. Any claim here about “what works” is a claim about view counts, nothing more.
  • These channels were hand-picked, not sampled at random. Findings describe these 93 channels, not YouTube.
  • Correlation, not causation. Creators who put numbers in titles differ in many other ways too.
  • Shorts are excluded — 8,048 of them. Their view distribution is entirely different; mixing them in manufactures correlations that really say “Shorts get views.”
  • Thresholds are Bonferroni-adjusted. Testing sixteen features at p<0.05 yields roughly one false positive by chance alone.
  • Survivorship. Deleted and private videos are invisible to the API and absent here.
